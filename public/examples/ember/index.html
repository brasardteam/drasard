(01) The room

One hearth. Fourteen seats. A menu that changes with the tide.

Ember opened in 2019 on a working dock, with a single oak-burning hearth and a rule we've never broken: everything passes through the flame. The catch comes off the boats five minutes away; the greens come from twelve raised beds behind the room. Between the two, there's the fire — and a counter close enough to feel it work.

(03) Story

A fire that started on a boat.

Before there was a room, a hearth, or a name, there was a driftwood fire on the shingle and whatever the nets brought up.

Mara Voss & Sen Ito — chef and forager, partners at the pass since 2016.

Ember started as a driftwood fire on a shingle beach, after the boats came in. Mara used to grill the day's leftover catch for anyone who helped haul the nets; Sen brought herbs from a window box and a bottle of something cold. People started showing up before the boats did.

In 2019 we found a room on Coal Dock Lane — an old net store with blackened beams and a floor that had already survived a hundred years of salt. We built the hearth first and the kitchen around it. There's no gas line, no electric grill, no freezer. If the fire isn't lit, we're not really open.

The menu is written each morning after the boats land and the garden's picked. Some nights there's langoustine; some nights there's excellent leeks. Fourteen seats, one sitting that never really ends, and a counter close enough to feel the heat on your forearms while you eat.

You don't give the fire instructions. You learn to cook alongside it.
Mara Voss — chef & founder

The way it works

From tide to table.

01

The boats

We buy whole, direct, and at dawn — whatever the day-boats land at the pier before the market opens. If it isn't swimming this morning, it isn't on the board tonight.

02

The coals

Oak and birch, burned down for two hours before service. Every dish passes over, under, or into it — even the ice cream gets its honey charred.

03

The counter

Fourteen seats, one hearth, no walls between you and the pass. Dinner takes as long as it takes — the fire doesn't rush, and neither do we.
