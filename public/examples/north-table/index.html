A big table, a hot fire and a short list of neighbours.

North Table is a neighbourhood restaurant built around a simple belief: food tastes better when it is shared, and better still when it has travelled a short way to reach you.

We cook with what Latvia gives us in each season. Roots in winter, greens in June, mushrooms and berries when the forest decides. Most of it goes near the fire, and none of it goes far from where it was grown.

Plates arrive in the middle of the table. Pass them around, take more bread, stay for another bottle.

The kitchen

Photograph: a crate of root vegetables

Vegetables, grains, eggs and dairy from farms we have visited. If it can be grown within two hours of Riga, we don't buy it from further away.

Herring, pike-perch and smelt from the coast. Rye, barley and caraway from the fields. Sea buckthorn, lingonberry and juniper from the dunes and woods.

A wood-fired hearth is the heart of the room. We burn seasoned birch and alder, cook directly over embers and let everything rest by the fire before it reaches your table.

Sauerkraut, kvass, pickled mushrooms and lacto-fermented hot sauce, all made in our back pantry. They bring acidity and depth to dishes that are otherwise simple.

We write the menu on Monday and change it when the produce does. A new potato in June will not look like a potato in November, and the plate should reflect that.

Photograph: flames in the open hearth

Open fire

Fire is the oldest way to cook, and the one we trust most. It gives bark to a chicken skin, a char to a carrot and a smokiness that no pan can copy.

Our hearth is lit at 14:00 and burns until we close. Come early and watch the cooks work the coals.
