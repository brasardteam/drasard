The Baltic pantry
Notes from the kitchen

The Baltic pantry

Every course at Aurelia begins long before service, in jars, crocks and curing boxes. The Baltic climate makes preserving a necessity, and we have come to see it as a creative tool rather than a constraint.

June beetroot is pickled in its own juices. Autumn mushrooms are dried and ground into a savoury dust. Rye bread that has gone past its best becomes the base of a dark, malty sauce. Smoke arrives from alder and juniper, never from a bottle.

Our pantry shelves are lined with things that will taste different in six months than they do today. That slow change is part of the menu. A guest who visits in March and again in October will meet the same kitchen with a very different voice.

Inside the Aurelia kitchen
A morning with the team

Inside the Aurelia kitchen

At 07:00 the kitchen is almost silent. Bread has been proved overnight, fish arrives from the coast by nine and the first stock of the day is already reducing.

There are six of us, and each person owns a station. One cook looks after the cold plates, one the fire, and one prepares every sauce. The chef tastes everything, often twice. Nothing leaves the pass without being approved by a spoon.

By four, the team gathers around a long table for staff meal. It is the only moment of the day when nobody is working, and it is where most of our best ideas begin. Then the doors open, the lights drop and the first guest is shown to their table.

Riga after dark
Where we go when the kitchen closes

Riga after dark

Riga is a city that rewards a late walk. The Art Nouveau facades on Alberta iela glow under street lamps, and the old town goes quiet enough that you can hear your own footsteps on the cobbles.

Our favourite nightcap is a small glass of Riga Black Balsam on the bar of a hotel we won't name, and our favourite late supper is a bowl of soup at a counter near the market. After service, the team often walks along the river towards Andrejsala to watch the lights on the water.

If you are dining with us, arrive a little early. Walk through Kronvalda Park, order a coffee, and let the evening slow down. Dinner tastes better when you are not in a hurry.
