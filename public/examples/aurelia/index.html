N° 01 · La Casa

A house of fire and patience.

  • Est. MCMLXXXVII
  • Posti 34 seats
  • Fuoco one wood fire
  • Guida una stella

Thirty-four seats gathered around a single oak fire, in a house that has been feeding this street since long before we thought to give it a name.

The building was an olive press until 1987, when Aurelia Marchetti traded her last harvest for the deed. The presses came out; the hearth went in. Three generations later the same oak burns in the same fireplace, and the menu is still written each afternoon — after the market, never before.

Chef Elena Marchetti cooks the way her grandmother did: over embers, with patience, and with whatever the morning trusted her with. Nothing foamed. Nothing was deconstructed. The fire is the only technique this kitchen has ever needed — everything else is just attention.

Elena Marchetti — third generation, keeper of the fire

N° 05 · L'Ordine — Da Asporto

The fire, to take home.

Every dish on tonight's menu travels — packed in copper-lined tins, sealed with the house wax, each with a small card telling you exactly how to wake it over your own flame.

Delivery goes out by bicycle, within the Old Quarter only — the food should still be breathing when it reaches you. Or collect from the door, where the lantern is lit from six o'clock.

Wines travel too, wrapped in the evening's newspaper. Orders are confirmed by telephone within fifteen minutes; the fire decides the rest.
